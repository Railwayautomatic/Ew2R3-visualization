FAQ

Where does E = ω²R³ come from?

It is the worldview-correct form of Kepler’s third law expressed through angular velocity, published in 1619 — 407 years ago. For bodies orbiting the same central body, a₁³/T₁² = a₂³/T₂². Since ω = 2π/T, the same relation can be written as ω²R³ = constant; this project denotes that constant by E. The E of each central celestial body is calculated from the observed orbital parameters of other bodies orbiting it: planets for the Sun, satellites for a planet.

What is E?

E is calculated as E = ω²R³ from an observed orbit. It has units m³/s² and is numerically comparable to the conventional gravitational parameter μ = GM for the corresponding central system.

[G·M] = m³kg·s² · kg = m³/s² — the kilogram cancels

Does the calculation use a planetary mass or G?

The operational calculation on this site uses orbital period and orbital radius. It does not require entering a mass or the universal gravitational constant.

Does a planet’s orbit around the Sun calculate the planet’s E?

No. That orbit primarily returns the parameter of the central Sun/two-body system. A planet’s own E is obtained from satellites orbiting that planet or another independently sourced value.

Is the E-field visualization an observed photograph?

No. It is an interactive model derived from the project’s equations and stated visualization conventions. Observed orbital data, derived values, and proposed interpretation are labelled separately.

Is the work peer-reviewed?

The listed works are public preprints. The site does not describe them as accepted journal publications unless that status is independently confirmed.

Why support an open project?

Support funds continued visualization, documentation, reproducible calculations, translations, and independent testing. Public papers and the core model remain accessible without a paywall.
